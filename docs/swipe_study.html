Can swipe-keyboard decoding get bare-desk typing to 100%?

One phone camera above a bare desk turns hand motion into text. Blind test 2 read 82% of words. This study checks whether the trick behind Gboard and SwiftKey swipe typing — matching a finger's path against every word in your vocabulary — closes the rest of the gap.

Verdict Not with today's camera signal. The path helps only when the typing finger is known — and from one camera it isn't.

On your keyboard recordings, a word's finger path picks the right word 96% of the time if we know which finger typed each letter. Guessing the finger drops that to 32%, and on the desk the path adds nothing measurable. Most remaining errors are words the camera evidence itself gets wrong, so the next gains must come from better evidence: more of your own labelled desk typing, plus a suggestion bar.

The swipe idea, measured

A finger path identifies a word only when you know the finger

Word top-1 from a SHARK²-style location scorer with a word-frequency prior, 728 typed words, leave-one-session-out. With 10% or 20% of taps missed or doubled, even the true-finger case falls to 68% and 45%.

On the desk

The same scorer added nothing measurable. An apparent +13–18 point gain turned out to be a leak: it came from knowing each word's letter count, and a constant score reproduced it exactly. With realistic, label-free inputs the fusion equals the camera model alone.

Vocabulary-only decoding

Restricting output to your 35.8k-word vocabulary brings “claude”, “codex” and “best” in at rank 1 whenever the camera supports them. “sorry”, “bifi”, “by”, “wonder”, “does” and “deceived” never appear in the top 50 under any setting.

What the literature says

No published system reaches 100% without a pick step

  • Swipe matching collapses on noisy surfaces. SHARK² drops to 42% on surface traces; neural swipe decoders reach 83–94% on phones with millions of training swipes.
  • Closest systems to ours (typing on a flat surface decoded from hand tracking — Richardson 2020, StegoType 2024, TouchInsight 2024) reach about 2–3% character errors with an hour of per-user data or hundreds of typists, roughly 88–95% of words.
  • Every ≥99% result (ATK, TOAST, TapType) uses touch or depth sensors, in-vocabulary prompts, or lets the user pick from candidates.
  • What transfers best: sentence-level decoding with a language model (we already do this), your own intent-labelled data, name biasing with a letter-by-letter escape, and a top-3 suggestion bar.

Realistic targets

Our estimate: about 88–92% of words fully automatic after the data step, and 95–98% with one tap on a suggestion. A guaranteed 100% fully automatic isn't supported by our data or by any published system.

How we'll know

Freeze everything, then a fresh blind test 3 of at least 150 words with the answer hash recorded before decoding. Report words correct with confidence intervals and every error sorted by type.

What's needed from you

One calibration session and one blind test

About 40–70 minutes total, phone above the desk as before: prompted phrases covering your most-used words and names, some natural typing, then a final blind test with at least 150 words you choose.

Caveats: one user, 250 labelled desk words across three sessions; blind test 1 was seen during development; some word-beam settings were inspected on all sets. Data & code: results/swipe/, phase0/analysis/swipe_*.py, docs/swipe_literature_notes.md, docs/SPEC_word_decoding.md.
